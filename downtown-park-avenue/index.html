Homes and apartments near the center.

Residences within a short walk of a commercial core face a slightly different set of questions. Does the unit share a wall with a restaurant or a storage room? Is the trash area shared? Do pipes or ducts run from a neighboring space? We ask all three because each describes a route into the home, and none depends on knowing the block.

  • Shared walls, ceilings and floors, plus any gaps around pipes that cross them.
  • Trash and recycling enclosures within a few steps of your door.
  • Plants, vines or awnings that touch the roof or siding.
  • Sheds, bike storage and garages that hold stored items.

Timing visits around opening hours.

Daytime work in a busy shop disturbs customers, and night work needs a key holder. The dispatcher asks about your hours, and a technician can arrange an early or late slot where the schedule has space. For homes, evening visits are common, because many people are away during the day.

Habits that lower the odds.

Break down cardboard promptly and keep dry goods off the floor. Close dock and delivery doors when nobody is using them. Report droppings, gnawing and sightings the same day, so a problem found in a week is not a problem found in a month.

Winter Park's warm weather keeps rodents active year-round, so these habits do not switch off for a season. If you are unsure whether a sign is rodent-related, phone the desk and describe it.

Do this today.

  • Keep children and dogs out of whichever room shows the signs..
  • Wear gloves before you handle anything stained or damp..
  • Glue boards catch lizards and songbirds as eagerly as rodents, so skip them..
  • Snap a few pictures before tidying, since the mess is evidence..

Put the broom down before dialing.

Return to this checklist whenever pellets, urine stains or shredded bedding turn up inside.

  • Dust is how the risk travels, so nothing dry gets brushed, blown or sucked up until it has been soaked.
  • Thirty minutes of airing comes before any cleanup in an enclosed area, according to the CDC.
  • Disposable gloves suit a one-time cleanup, while thick rubber ones suit bigger jobs and can be washed afterward.
  • Spray droppings and urine spots with disinfectant, or with bleach cut with water, until they glisten, then allow roughly five minutes.
  • Throw the bag away with ordinary garbage and rinse any reusable tools you handled.
  • Should something feel wrong afterward, visit a doctor and describe the cleanup so they can weigh it.
