How the pieces fit together.

Most households follow the same path. An inspection names the animal and prices the work. Trapping removes what is inside, sealing closes the way in and cleanup or repair follows when needed.

Nobody has to book every step. If you only need a vent cover replaced or a smell traced, say so, and the estimate shows that and nothing else.

Official references.

Complete instructions and product limits sit on the public pages that follow.

  • The CDC on lowering hantavirus risk: Keeping well around rodents through sealing, then trapping, then cleaning.
  • CDC rodent waste cleanup steps: Gloves, airing, damp wiping and the disposal of pellets and nest scraps, one step at a time.

Questions about the service list.

Is there a page for emergency calls?

Urgent help is woven into each page and not split into its own, and the desk answers through every hour. A same-day visit is offered when a slot is open.

Which service should I start with?

An inspection is the safest start when you are unsure. It names the animal and lists the work in order, with a price on each line.

Can I book just one part of the job?

Yes. Sealing, cleanup, repair and trapping can each be booked on their own, though sealing before repair prevents rework.

Do you serve businesses?

Yes. The commercial page covers delivery doors, shared walls, dumpsters and written notes after each visit.
