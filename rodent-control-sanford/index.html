What prompts a {T} call.

The first call usually starts with a minor clue that is easy to overlook.

  • Droppings on the attic insulation.
  • Noises inside a wall after the house goes quiet.
  • A hole under the sink where a pipe enters.
  • A pet that stares at one spot in the wall.

A year of rodent weather.

Wet weather rearranges rodent habits more than it removes them. Flooded burrows send animals looking for dry ground, and a gap in a wall can look like a good place to wait out the rain.

Between the call and the visit.

  • Pull the door shut on the loudest room and give it a wide berth until the visit..
  • Pellets stay where they landed, because a broom stirs them into the air you breathe..
  • Loose poison from the shelf can send a dying animal into a duct or a wall cavity..
  • Jot down the clock time each time you hear movement above you..

Put the broom down before dialing.

Following these steps takes no phone call, since they mirror the advice agencies give to anyone who spots droppings indoors.

  • Skip the broom and the shop vacuum. Dry droppings crumble, and anything that stirs them sends fine particles into your breathing space.
  • Do not walk into a long-closed storage area and start working, because airing it for thirty minutes comes first.
  • Hand protection comes first and hand washing comes last, with the cleanup in between.
  • The CDC bleach recipe is one part bleach to nine parts water, mixed fresh for the job.
  • Put soiled paper towels and any carcass into one plastic bag, handled with gloves or tongs, then knot it and set it in the regular bin.
  • Pest work is our limit, and if you feel ill after touching rodent waste, a clinician is the right call.

Government pages we relied on.

Every precaution in this article comes from published government pages, not from a firm that profits from fear.

  • Rodent cleanup page at cdc.gov: The air, soak and bag routine that this page's cleanup advice borrows from.
  • CDC advice on keeping rodents out: CDC explanation of hantavirus, with tips for keeping rodents away from living spaces and cars.
