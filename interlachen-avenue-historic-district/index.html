Why altered buildings need joint-by-joint checks.

Lakes, oaks and the approach to the roof.

Being near a lake edge or under mature trees can change how rodents approach a building. A limb resting on a roof hands roof rats a bridge, and leaf litter banked against a foundation lets mice approach under cover. These are general facts about lots with trees and water, not claims about any specific property.

  • Check limbs and vines that reach the roof, soffits or chimney.
  • Look at the base of walls for leaf litter, mulch and groundcover pressed against siding.
  • Inspect any vent, screen or louver for bent or missing metal.
  • Note where gutters, downspouts or hoses create damp spots beside the house.

Rodents move in fast, so phoning early tends to be cheaper than fixing chewed wiring later.

Timing and seasons.

Rodents stay active through central Florida's mild winters, so there is no off season to wait for. Storm debris, a fallen limb or a shifted vent cover can open a route overnight, so check the roof edge and the foundation after heavy weather.

Before you call.

Leave droppings alone, keep children and pets away from affected rooms and take photographs of what you see. For old attics or crawl spaces that have not been opened in a long time, do not go in without protection. The CDC box lower on this page explains the safe way to handle waste.

Mention any features you want protected, such as original trim, plaster or masonry. The more the dispatcher knows, the better the first visit can be planned.

Before you call, a cleanup primer.

Rodent waste can carry germs, so careful cleanup is slow, damp and quiet. Each step traces to the CDC pages named at the foot of this article.

  • Dust is how the risk travels, so nothing dry gets brushed, blown or sucked up until it has been soaked.
  • Before cleanup, crack every opening in the space and busy yourself elsewhere for half an hour.
  • Disposable gloves suit a one-time cleanup, while thick rubber ones suit bigger jobs and can be washed afterward.
  • The CDC bleach recipe is one part bleach to nine parts water, mixed fresh for the job.
  • Everything touched by the cleanup, from towels to gloves, rides to the outdoor bin in a knotted bag.
  • Feeling sick after handling rodent waste is a matter for a physician, so tell them exactly what you touched.
