Before you call: what the CDC asks of any household.

Following these steps keeps dust low while you wait for a technician.

  • Even a gentle swish of a dust mop can lift old pellets into the air, so no dry tool should start the job.
  • Fresh air does the first part of the job: half an hour with the openings wide and nobody in the room.
  • Bare skin should never meet pellets, urine spots or nesting scraps, so gloves go on first.
  • Spray generously, step back, give it a few minutes and then lift the mess with disposable towels.
  • One tied bag per cleanup session keeps things simple and keeps the waste contained.
  • Tell your doctor about any rodent contact if you fall ill within a few weeks, since we stick to pest control.
