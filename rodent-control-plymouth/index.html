Before you call, read this short list.

Calm, deliberate movements keep fine grit from drifting into the air you breathe.

  • Whatever you reach for first, put it back if it is dry: brooms, vacuums, compressed air and rags all count.
  • Fresh air does the first part of the job: half an hour with the openings wide and nobody in the room.
  • Disposable gloves suit a one-time cleanup, while thick rubber ones suit bigger jobs and can be washed afterward.
  • Spray generously, step back, give it a few minutes and then lift the mess with disposable towels.
  • Throw the bag away with ordinary garbage and rinse any reusable tools you handled.
  • A fever, muscle aches or short breath in the weeks after a cleanup are reasons to see a doctor and mention the rodent exposure, as we give no medical advice.
