Why cleanup is its own job.

Rodents shred insulation into nests, urinate in it and leave droppings by the hundreds. Compressed batts lose insulating value, stained areas hold odor and old waste can crumble into dust whenever someone steps nearby. Trapping removes the animals but leaves all of that in place.

In warm, humid weather the problem multiplies. Damp insulation grows musty, and droppings that stay for months release a smell that can find its way into rooms below through light fixtures and attic hatches. Cleanup resets the space.

Sequence of a cleanup visit.

  • Confirm that trapping and sealing are done and that no live animals remain.
  • Ventilate the space and set up containment for dust.
  • Wet down visible waste, then bag contaminated insulation and nest material.
  • Sanitize framing and decking, and treat odors at the source.
  • Vacuum with HEPA filtration after wet removal, and inspect the space again.

The order matters because dry removal throws dust into the air. Wetting first holds particles down, and the CDC box on this page explains the same logic for small household cleanups.

If wiring or ductwork was chewed during the infestation, damage repair handles it after cleanup, and the sealing visit keeps the new insulation from being reached again.

Timing around heat and storms.

Summer attics in central Florida can become punishing by midday, so crews schedule heavy removal for early hours when possible. After a heavy storm, check for roof leaks before cleanup begins, because wet insulation is heavier to remove and may need extra drying.

Older roofs and long-quiet attics.

Houses on tree-shaded lots can carry a particular risk, because limbs that have rubbed a roof for years may shift flashing and open small gaps. An attic that has not been opened in a long time may hold many seasons of layered material. If you are buying, selling or inheriting a house, mention it when you call, because an inspection before cleanup tells us how much material is involved.

Before the crew arrives.

Clear stored items from the path to the hatch, and keep pets out of the house during the work. Tell the dispatcher about any allergies or respiratory conditions in the household, since occupants may want to stay elsewhere while dust-producing steps are underway.

Holding steps.

  • Park a vehicle with debris under the hood until someone has checked it..
  • Carry grass seed, dog food and feeder mix indoors until this is sorted..
  • Bag any gnawed item and save it for the visit..
  • Locate the breaker box, in case a gnawed cable needs shutting off..

Before you call: handling droppings while keeping dust down.

Read each line twice before touching a thing, since a little patience is kind to your lungs.

  • Pellets that look harmless on a shelf release grit when scuffed, which is why dry cleanup is discouraged.
  • Do not walk into a long-closed storage area and start working, because airing it for thirty minutes comes first.
  • Peel the gloves off inside out when you are done, bag them if they are disposable and scrub with soap and water.
  • The CDC bleach recipe is one part bleach to nine parts water, mixed fresh for the job.
  • Throw the bag away with ordinary garbage and rinse any reusable tools you handled.
  • Keep an eye on how you feel for a few weeks, and phone your doctor if flu-like symptoms or labored breathing appear.

Cleanup questions from homeowners.

Why can't I just vacuum the attic myself?

A vacuum sends fine particles from dry droppings into the breathing zone. CDC guidance calls for damp methods and protective gear, so large jobs are handed to trained crews.

Do you replace insulation?

Removal and sanitizing are part of cleanup. Replacement is quoted as a separate line so you can compare it with other options.

How do I know the animals are gone first?

Cleanup follows trapping and sealing, and we confirm that no fresh signs are present before any material is removed.

Will the smell go away?

Taking out the cause along with the soiled insulation normally clears it. A dead animal inside a wall needs its own removal visit.

Is a crawl space handled the same way?

The steps are the same, though access is tighter and the crew wears protective gear. Moisture there gets extra attention.
