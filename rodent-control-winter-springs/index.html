How rodent trouble shows up indoors.

The first call usually starts with a minor clue that is easy to overlook.

  • A thump overhead just after dark.
  • Gnaw marks on a wire cover or a plastic pipe.
  • Greasy smears along the baseboard.
  • Shredded paper or insulation in a corner.

What the weather does to rodent habits.

There is no off season to wait out. Heat keeps animals foraging, rain moves them around and a cooled house stays inviting through hot afternoons.

Before you call, a cleanup primer.

Following these steps takes no phone call, since they mirror the advice agencies give to anyone who spots droppings indoors.

  • Whatever you reach for first, put it back if it is dry: brooms, vacuums, compressed air and rags all count.
  • Do not walk into a long-closed storage area and start working, because airing it for thirty minutes comes first.
  • Put rubber or plastic gloves on before touching anything stained, and finish by washing your hands with soap.
  • Wipe up wet waste with paper towels and never dry cloth, and keep soaking until nothing looks dusty.
  • Throw the bag away with ordinary garbage and rinse any reusable tools you handled.
  • Pest work is our limit, and if you feel ill after touching rodent waste, a clinician is the right call.
