The garage is a doorway, not a room.

A garage is the part of the house built with the fewest tight seals. The roll-up door never quite meets the floor, side seals harden in the sun and the track corners leave gaps a rat can use. Meanwhile the interior holds cardboard, rope, rags, pet food and a refrigerator humming in the corner.

Rodents treat that setting as a rest stop. They nest in boxes, run along the wall at floor level and cross into the house through the door to the kitchen or laundry room. Fixing the garage often fixes the whole house.

What gets checked and in what order.

  • The bottom seal and both side seals, tested with a flashlight from outside after dark if possible.
  • Track corners, where gaps open at the base of each rail.
  • The slab edge, looking for crumbled concrete or burrows.
  • Utility penetrations, such as water lines, conduit and the sleeve for the air conditioning drain.
  • The interior door to the house, including its sweep and the wall around it.

Droppings along baseboards and under shelving show the runs. A greasy smear low on the wall confirms it. We photograph the evidence before disturbing anything.

Reducing clutter does not remove a colony, but it exposes the path so trapping can be placed correctly, and sealing can finish the job.

Weather and the roll-up door.

Heat expands a metal door and cold contracts it, but Florida's real stress is humidity and sun. Rubber seals crack, brush strips wear flat and tracks loosen. After a hurricane warning or storm cleanup, check the bottom seal first, since flying debris often knocks it out of place.

Snap the pellets or the bite marks, then say so when you call, and the visit gets quicker.

Collections, hobby gear and long storage.

Garages in this part of Florida often hold more than cars: kayaks for the lakes, bicycles, gardening supplies, holiday decorations and family belongings that outlast several moves. Lake-chain living tends to bring boats and paddling gear, and those items sit in corners for months. Rodents settle behind anything that stays still. Rotate stored items every season, and tell us what is parked long-term.

Safe cleanup in a garage.

Open the doors and give it a half hour before you work, keep the vacuum put away and use damp methods only. The CDC box lower on this page lists each step in order. If the infestation is large or the garage has been shut for a long time, call us before starting.

Before you call, a few rules to protect yourself.

Return to this checklist whenever pellets, urine stains or shredded bedding turn up inside.

  • Leave powdery debris undisturbed until it is damp, because the CDC steers people away from sweeping and vacuuming for good reason.
  • Let a cross-breeze pass through for roughly half an hour, then go in, and skip the sniff test.
  • Bare skin should never meet pellets, urine spots or nesting scraps, so gloves go on first.
  • Disinfect the surface under and around the waste too, since urine travels beyond the visible spot.
  • A second bag around the first adds insurance if the plastic is thin or the load is heavy.
  • Pest work is our limit, and if you feel ill after touching rodent waste, a clinician is the right call.

Garage questions, answered briefly.

Why do rodents like garages so much?

They offer shelter, clutter and quiet, plus easy access under the door. A refrigerator running in the corner adds warmth, and pet food or birdseed adds a meal.

Are there basements in Florida houses?

Genuine basements are scarce around here. A laundry room, utility closet or space under the house usually serves the same purpose, and we inspect it the same way.

Should I throw out chewed boxes?

Photograph them first, then bag them with gloves on. Cardboard with droppings should be handled with the wet method from the CDC box on this page.

Will a door sweep alone solve it?

It helps, but gaps in the track, wall seams and utility sleeves also matter. We check all of them in one pass.

Can you work if the garage is packed?

Yes, though clearing a path along the walls speeds the work. We can suggest what to move before the visit.
