Tenants, landlords and shared systems.

Where several households share a roof, a hallway or a trash enclosure, treatment in one unit may not end the problem. Say who manages the building, and whether any neighbor has reported signs.

Landlords and managers can authorize sealing at shared walls and rooflines, which is where most multi-unit routes begin. We can document findings in writing so a manager can act.

A tree-by-tree look at access.

Landscaping that touches walls gives rodents cover as they test for gaps. Keep mulch a few inches below the siding line, pull shrubs back from the foundation and keep firewood or lumber raised and well clear of the house.

Central Florida seasons and your walls.

Because winters are mild, rodent activity does not drop the way it does in colder states. The calendar matters less than conditions: a storm, a fallen limb or a loose vent cover.

Put the broom down before dialing.

Thinking of tidying up before our crew arrives? Work through this list in order and stop at any point that feels unclear.

  • Skip the broom and the shop vacuum. Dry droppings crumble, and anything that stirs them sends fine particles into your breathing space.
  • Give a closed-up space a half hour of fresh air before you step inside to work, with doors open, windows open and people elsewhere.
  • Disposable gloves suit a one-time cleanup, while thick rubber ones suit bigger jobs and can be washed afterward.
  • Spray droppings and urine spots with disinfectant, or with bleach cut with water, until they glisten, then allow roughly five minutes.
  • Bagging keeps stray particles from drifting through the house on the way out.
  • Tell your doctor about any rodent contact if you fall ill within a few weeks, since we stick to pest control.
