What gets reported first.

None of the clues below is proof by itself. Two or three together usually are.

  • A new scratching sound after a storm.
  • Debris piled against a wall.
  • A garage door gasket that has shifted.
  • Droppings where none were found before.

Weather and rodents around Oviedo.

Storm season brings limbs, fronds and debris that can form bridges to a roof. After any big storm, walk the property and trim what touches the building.

Small steps for now.

  • Stay off the attic ladder and out of the crawl space until we get there, because disturbed insulation hangs in the air..
  • A nest is best left alone, because a startled mother can bolt deeper into the framing..
  • Know where your attic hatch and crawl-space door are, so nobody wastes time hunting for them..
  • Slide stored boxes away from the hatch only if lifting them is safe..

Before you call, a few rules to protect yourself.

Return to this checklist whenever pellets, urine stains or shredded bedding turn up inside.

  • A leaf blower in the garage is the worst idea on this list, and a household vacuum is a close second.
  • Do not walk into a long-closed storage area and start working, because airing it for thirty minutes comes first.
  • Plan to wash up well after every cleanup session, even if you wore gloves the whole time.
  • The CDC bleach recipe is one part bleach to nine parts water, mixed fresh for the job.
  • Everything touched by the cleanup, from towels to gloves, rides to the outdoor bin in a knotted bag.
  • A fever, muscle aches or short breath in the weeks after a cleanup are reasons to see a doctor and mention the rodent exposure, as we give no medical advice.
