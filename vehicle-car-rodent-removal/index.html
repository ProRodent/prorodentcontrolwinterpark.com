Why idle vehicles end up with nests.

An engine bay is warm after a drive, full of soft insulation and screened from wind and rain. A parked vehicle is quiet and stationary, which is exactly what a nesting rodent looks for. Add a bag of birdseed in the trunk or a half-eaten snack in the cabin and the vehicle becomes both home and pantry.

Cars in a garage can be at greater risk than cars on the street, because the garage already attracts rodents. A camper, boat trailer or golf cart in the corner of a storage area offers even more cover.

Where we look.

  • Air intake box and filter, where leaves and nest material often collect.
  • Cabin air filter and the blower housing.
  • Wiring harnesses, especially soft-coated types.
  • Underbody shields and insulation panels.
  • Trunk, spare tire well and any stored items.

Signs include droppings on top of the engine, shredded material in the air box, a musty smell from the vents and gnaw marks on hoses. We photograph what we find and bag the material for disposal.

Parked longer than a few weeks.

Seasonal residents and travelers often leave cars parked for extended periods. Before leaving, clean the interior, remove food and consider a visible reminder, such as a card on the steering wheel, to check under the hood on return. If you come back to a smell or a warning light, call before driving.

Cars near lakes and wooded streets.

A driveway beside a wooded edge or a lake path offers cover close to a vehicle. Park away from shrubs and brush when possible, and keep the area under the car clear of debris. On a street with mature trees, falling leaves and seed pods can collect around tires and the undercarriage, so a quick check after storms makes sense.

A quick check you can do.

Open the hood and look for debris, droppings and shredded material. Smell the cabin vents. Check for chewed items in the trunk. Do not clean heavy contamination yourself, and follow the CDC steps if you handle waste.

Ahead of the technician.

  • Stay off the attic ladder and out of the crawl space until we get there, because disturbed insulation hangs in the air..
  • A nest is best left alone, because a startled mother can bolt deeper into the framing..
  • Know where your attic hatch and crawl-space door are, so nobody wastes time hunting for them..
  • Slide stored boxes away from the hatch only if lifting them is safe..

Before you call: handling droppings while keeping dust down.

Thinking of tidying up before our crew arrives? Work through this list in order and stop at any point that feels unclear.

  • A leaf blower in the garage is the worst idea on this list, and a household vacuum is a close second.
  • Garages, sheds, attics and spare rooms that stayed shut tend to hold stale air, so open them and wait about thirty minutes.
  • Bare skin should never meet pellets, urine spots or nesting scraps, so gloves go on first.
  • The CDC bleach recipe is one part bleach to nine parts water, mixed fresh for the job.
  • Put soiled paper towels and any carcass into one plastic bag, handled with gloves or tongs, then knot it and set it in the regular bin.
  • A fever, muscle aches or short breath in the weeks after a cleanup are reasons to see a doctor and mention the rodent exposure, as we give no medical advice.

Vehicle questions answered.

Why would a rodent nest in my car?

Engine bays stay warm after driving and offer sheltered voids around hoses and insulation. A vehicle that sits for a while is quiet, which suits nesting.

Do you repair the damage?

We locate and remove the nest and report damage. Wiring, hoses and sensors are repaired by a qualified mechanic or an auto electrician.

Can rodents damage a golf cart?

Yes. Carts and similar vehicles often sit unused, and exposed wiring under the seat or in the battery bay is easy to reach.

Is it safe to start the car?

If you suspect gnawed wiring, avoid starting it until it has been checked. A damaged harness can create electrical problems.

How do I prevent a repeat?

Keep food out of the vehicle, close garage gaps, move storage away from the car and check under the hood periodically if it sits.
