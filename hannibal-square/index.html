Where our knowledge ends.

According to the Hannibal Square Heritage Center page, Hannibal Square, known as the west side, was officially founded in 1881, and Wikipedia's wording is that its residents provided labor that helped build the town. We pass that along carefully. For rodent work, the date and the story matter in one small way: they tell us nothing about your walls, so when you call, give the street address, say which parts of the house were added later and let us inspect those joints first.

Past that, we say nothing about streets, businesses or the ages of houses. The name is a common label for the area, not a surveyed border, and we book by address. Every property is treated as what it is when the technician arrives.

Trees, lakes and the roofline.

Winter Park sits among central Florida lakes, and many lots carry mature trees such as live oaks, often with Spanish moss and palms nearby. A limb that touches a roof gives roof rats an easy bridge, and dead palm fronds supply shelter and fruit. Neither is a flaw in a lot. Both are routes worth checking before any trap is chosen.

  • Look for limbs, vines or fronds within a few feet of the roof edge.
  • Check soffit panels and vent covers for bent or missing pieces.
  • Walk the foundation for burrows, loose soil and chewed screens.
  • Note any fallen fruit, feeder seed or pet food left out overnight.

Those four checks usually reveal whether the activity is overhead, at ground level or both, and that decides whether we start with the roofline or the slab.

Weather and timing.

Rodents do not take a winter break in this climate. Afternoon storms from early summer push animals out of flooded burrows, and a cooled house stays inviting through hot afternoons. After heavy rain, walk the property and look for fresh holes, shifted vent covers and debris pressed against the walls.

What to do while you wait for us.

Leave droppings undisturbed, keep children and pets away from the room with signs and photograph anything you find. Skip loose bait and glue boards. The CDC box lower on this page explains how to handle waste safely if cleanup cannot wait.

When you call, mention the room, the time of day and what you heard or smelled. A short, specific description gets the first visit moving faster than any long story.

Before you call, a few rules to protect yourself.

Following these steps keeps dust low while you wait for a technician.

  • Put your energy into wetting things down and not into sweeping them up, because wet first is the whole point of this step.
  • Give a closed-up space a half hour of fresh air before you step inside to work, with doors open, windows open and people elsewhere.
  • Bare skin should never meet pellets, urine spots or nesting scraps, so gloves go on first.
  • Soaking comes before wiping every time, since the liquid ties down the dust and keeps the wipe-up gentle.
  • One tied bag per cleanup session keeps things simple and keeps the waste contained.
  • Tell your doctor about any rodent contact if you fall ill within a few weeks, since we stick to pest control.
