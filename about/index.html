What this service is.

Winter Park Pro Rodent Control takes rodent calls for Winter Park, Florida and for fifteen nearby places in Orange and Seminole counties. Someone is on the phone at every hour, so a person picks up whether trouble starts at lunch or at 3 a.m.

We do not claim a long history, awards or special credentials on this site, and we do not show reviews or ratings. What we can describe truthfully is how a rodent job is done, from inspection to the last trap.

How a job works.

  • You phone the desk and describe what you saw, heard or smelled.
  • The dispatcher asks for your street address and offers the earliest open slot.
  • A technician inspects, names the animal and writes down a price per step.
  • Trapping, sealing, cleanup or repair follow in the order that prevents rework.
  • A return visit checks traps and seals on dates you were given.

About the address in the footer.

The footer lists 7024 Brewer Avenue, Winter Park, FL 32789 as a citation address. Nobody is on duty there, and it is not a shop or an office you can visit. That ZIP code is not among our coverage codes, which are 32792, 32793 and 32790.

Where the information comes from.

Local facts on this site, such as the history of Winter Park and its landmarks, come from public encyclopedia pages and are used sparingly, always tied to a practical step in a house or shop. Safety guidance comes from the CDC and the EPA, which are linked at the bottom of every article.

How we chose the places on the list.

Coverage is drawn by ZIP code. We kept every code within about fifteen miles of the middle of Winter Park by straight line, and we dropped any that fell just past that mark. The result is fifteen places, from Maitland at roughly two miles to Plymouth near fifteen.

Some codes in our source data had no city name. Instead of guessing, we paired those rows by ZIP code and checked their positions against a second geocoder. The areas page explains which pages that affects.

What goes on a written estimate.

  • The animal, as the technician identified it.
  • Each entry point found, with the material that will close it.
  • Individual prices for trapping work, sealing work, cleanup and repairs.
  • The dates of return visits to check traps and seals.
  • Anything we noticed but did not recommend, such as a roof leak for a roofer.

You approve line by line. If a step is not needed at your property, it does not appear, and you are never asked to accept the whole list to get the first part done.

How the pages here are written.

Each article, service page and place page is written to be read by someone standing in a hallway with a flashlight. We keep sentences short, name the limits of what we know and send you to the phone when only a technician can answer.

Honest limits.

We cannot see inside your walls from here, so every page tells you to describe the signs and let a technician confirm. We do not give medical advice, and we do not promise a result before an inspection.

Pages behind the safety notes.

Each safety remark rests on federal guidance, and a line beside every link says what it covers.

  • CDC: hantavirus and prevention: Shut the entrances first, and only then deal gently with old droppings and nests.
  • EPA page on rodenticide products: Steps the EPA took to protect kids, pets and non-target animals from poison bait.
