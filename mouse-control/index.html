Why kitchens attract mice.

A house mouse is fussy about three things: a dry hiding place, food within a short dash and a covered path linking the two. The hollow behind a pantry wall provides all three together. So does the corner of a garage where bins of seasonal decor sit against drywall.

Central Florida removes the usual off season. Humid warmth lingers for most of the year, and afternoon storms from early summer push animals up out of soggy ground. Under big oaks on shaded streets, leaf litter and shrubs often rest against foundations and hand mice a covered approach, although a bare lot guarantees nothing either way.

Most people discover mice late, because the animals work after the house goes quiet. After the house settles, pause beside the pantry, the utility closet and the garage wall the kitchen leans on.

Evidence first, traps second.

The visit opens at the freshest evidence and widens in rings. A scatter of new pellets, an oily scuff along the baseboard or a cereal box with a gnawed corner each become a pin on a rough map, and the pins lead to a hole. A trap set without that map takes a few mice and leaves the doorway standing.

  • Under both sinks first, then the gaps beside the stove and refrigerator, then the utility closet.
  • Garage corners: the foot of the door, its rubber seal and the seam between wall and slab.
  • Up top: the attic hatch, the roof vents and every spot where a pipe or cable pierces the roof deck.
  • Down low: foundation vents or skirting panels, especially on the wetter side of the house.

Catch first, close second.

Depending on the room we use snap traps or closed stations, set along the walls where mice actually travel and well away from where children and pets play. Whiskers guide a mouse along walls and it hesitates in the open, so a trap tight to the baseboard outperforms one parked mid-room.

Sealing is scheduled after the first catches show which entrances are still in use. Steel wool alone does not last, because teeth pull it out, so we pack each opening with mesh and then flash or caulk over it. The exclusion page names the materials and the trapping page covers the stations.

Habits for the days of waiting.

Leave droppings undisturbed for now and keep the broom in the closet, since sweeping launches dust. The CDC box lower on this page describes the wet method for when cleanup time comes, and any bait should stay out of the house.

In a damp climate, packaging matters. Pour pantry staples and pet kibble into containers with snap lids, and take the water dish up overnight. Those steps will not evict a mouse already inside, but they stop the buffet from recruiting more.

Before you call, read this short list.

Calm, deliberate movements keep fine grit from drifting into the air you breathe.

  • Skip the broom and the shop vacuum. Dry droppings crumble, and anything that stirs them sends fine particles into your breathing space.
  • Garages, sheds, attics and spare rooms that stayed shut tend to hold stale air, so open them and wait about thirty minutes.
  • Gloves protect the hands, and hands touch faces, so keep the gloves on until the last bag is tied.
  • The CDC bleach recipe is one part bleach to nine parts water, mixed fresh for the job.
  • Dead animals stay off bare hands, and a gloved hand or a shovel moves them into the bag.
  • Should something feel wrong afterward, visit a doctor and describe the cleanup so they can weigh it.

Mouse questions from Winter Park callers.

How small a hole can a mouse really use?

About the diameter of a pencil, or a bit more. Look beside drain pipes, behind the dryer, along door trim and at any spot a cable pokes through the siding.

Do mice slow down in a Florida summer?

No. A cool house tempts them in July as much as in January, and a week of storms can drive them off soaked ground. A gap left open in spring often becomes the summer door.

Can I use hardware store bait while I wait?

Ask the technician first. A poisoned mouse frequently dies inside a wall void, and the odor that follows is a worse headache than the mouse was.

How long until the noise stops?

It depends on the head count and on how many entrances are in use. After the walk-through we give a timeline we can stand behind, and before it any number would be a guess.

Is a call at 3 a.m. all right?

Yes. Staff answer through the night, and if the day's schedule has a gap we offer a visit that same day.
